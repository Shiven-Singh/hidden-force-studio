# The Rhythm of Maple Street

**Logline.** When a series of pedestrian crossing lights begin to fail in a decaying countdown sequence, an eight-year-old autistic girl must use her unique perception of patterns to warn the neighborhood before the busy school crossing goes completely dark.

**Trait causality.** Aanya's autistic focus on environmental rhythms and sequences allows her to detect a decaying signal timing pattern that non-disabled townspeople dismiss as a minor glitch.

## 1. The Quiet Goal

Aanya set out with a simple personal mission: to walk to the library courtyard and place her perfect smooth gray pebble on the stone bench.

*Trait in play:* Aanya's intense appreciation for quiet spaces and her sensory focus on the tactile feel of her totem pebble drive her goal.

## 2. The Missed Beat

At the Oak Street crossing, Aanya notices the flashing orange hand signal blinks only three times instead of its usual four, cutting the walk short.

*Trait in play:* Aanya's sharp eye for environmental sequences allows her to immediately spot the rhythm change that her mom overlooks.

## 3. Mapping the Grid

In her quiet bedroom, Aanya catalogs the flashing sequences of the neighborhood lights, suspecting a broader pattern is at play.

*Trait in play:* She uses her systematic thinking and love of grids to map out the mathematical intervals of the traffic lights.

## 4. The Elm Street Glitch

The next morning, Aanya witnesses the Elm Street signal drop to only two blinks and pulls her classmate Toby back just in time as a car rushes through.

*Trait in play:* Her understanding of the sequence decay lets her anticipate the dangerously short crossing window before it happens.

## 5. The Unheard Warning

Aanya tries to warn the school crossing guard, Mr. Henderson, about the failing countdown, but he brushes it off as a simple computer glitch.

*Trait in play:* Aanya speaks with absolute, literal clarity about the math, but the adult misinterprets her systematic focus.

## 6. Sensory Storm

The overwhelming noise and flashing lights of the busy intersection trigger a sensory overload, which Aanya manages with her headphones and her totem pebble.

*Trait in play:* Aanya's sensory sensitivity is represented visually, and she uses her self-soothing strategies to ground herself.

## 7. The Critical Prediction

Back home, Aanya calculates that the main school crossing on Maple Street will lose its warning blinks entirely by tomorrow morning at 8:15 AM.

*Trait in play:* She relies on her unwavering trust in mathematical patterns to find the exact moment of failure.

## 8. Building the Proof

Aanya builds a working cardboard and LED model of the intersection to show the adults the physical rhythm of the failure.

*Trait in play:* She channels her focus into creating a physical, visual representation of the pattern she sees so clearly.

## 9. The Demonstration

On Maple Street, Aanya sets up her model and presents her findings to Officer Davis as the school bell approaches.

*Trait in play:* Aanya's determination and strong moral compass compel her to advocate for the safety of her peers.

## 10. The Zero Count

At exactly 8:14 AM, the Maple Street signal fails just as Aanya's model predicted, allowing her to stop Toby from stepping into the path of an oncoming truck.

*Trait in play:* Aanya's precise timing and quick action resolve the danger, proving her pattern-reading skills to the community.

## 11. The Completed Pattern

With the city alerted and the crossing safe, Aanya finally reaches the quiet library courtyard to place her pebble in peace.

*Trait in play:* Aanya remains exactly who she is, finding comfort and joy in the quiet, predictable rhythm of her completed task.
