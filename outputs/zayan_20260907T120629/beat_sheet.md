# The Thirty-Second Shuffle

**Logline.** When a school's experimental sliding-wall system goes haywire and creates a shifting maze, a nine-year-old boy with ADHD must use his fast-tracking brain to navigate the thirty-second reshuffle and save his classmates.

**Trait causality.** The shifting maze reshuffles every thirty seconds on multiple axes simultaneously, making it impossible for a neurotypical person focusing on a single path to navigate, meaning only Zayan's ADHD brain—which naturally tracks multiple moving patterns at once—can find the exit.

## 1. The Cosmic Goal

Zayan is stuck in afternoon study hall. He wants one thing: to get his confiscated Cosmic Commander sticker book back from the trophy cabinet before the weekend.

*Trait in play:* Zayan is bouncing in his seat, unable to keep his feet still, showing his high energy and focus on multiple things at once.

## 2. The Broken Feeling

During a brief recess, Zayan tries to play handball with Dev and Priya, but his attention shifts to a passing bird. He drops the ball, and they walk away, leaving him feeling isolated.

*Trait in play:* Zayan experiences low self-esteem and friendlessness, explaining his Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder diagnosis and how it makes him feel 'broken'.

## 3. The Clocktower Clack

Back inside, the school's newly installed 'Modular Corridor' system, designed to save space, goes completely haywire. A massive mechanical clack echoes through the building.

*Trait in play:* Zayan's fast pattern recognition immediately registers the changing rhythm of the school's ventilation and floor vibrations.

## 4. The Maze Appears

The walls of the school slide on tracks, cutting off the classroom. The hallway turns into a labyrinth of shifting blue and orange panels.

*Trait in play:* While the teacher, Mr. Henderson, panics, Zayan's mind begins scanning the room for escape routes, showing his funny-under-pressure attitude.

## 5. Thirty-Second Warning

They discover the walls reshuffle on a strict thirty-second timer. Mr. Henderson gets separated from the kids by a sliding solid barrier.

*Trait in play:* Zayan uses his hyperactive energy to climb a desk and peer over a partition, tracking the movement of three corridors at once.

## 6. The Grid in the Mind

Dev and Priya panic as the walls start to close in. Zayan realizes the chaos has a rhythm. His brain, which usually tracks too many things, is built for this.

*Trait in play:* Zayan explains how his ADHD brain sees every possibility at once, allowing him to map the shifting maze in real-time.

## 7. The Leap of Faith

Zayan convinces Dev and Priya to follow his lead. He snaps his rubber band to ground himself and takes the first leap through a closing gap.

*Trait in play:* Zayan snaps his rubber band totem to focus his mind, then acts fearlessly to lead the others.

## 8. The Thirty-Second Dash

Zayan guides Dev and Priya through three rapid wall-shifts, counting down the seconds perfectly. They dodge sliding lockers and moving water fountains.

*Trait in play:* Zayan's fast pattern recognition allows him to calculate the exact path before the walls slide.

## 9. The Trophy Cabinet Dilemma

They reach the main lobby. The exit is open, but the final thirty-second shift is starting. The trophy cabinet with Zayan's sticker book is on the opposite side of a sliding wall.

*Trait in play:* Zayan's strong desire for his personal goal collides with the time pressure, causing him to think of a high-speed, dual-focus solution.

## 10. The Double-Shift Solution

Zayan uses a sliding wall as a bridge. He grabs his sticker book and pulls Dev and Priya through the closing front doors just as the maze locks shut.

*Trait in play:* Zayan's brain tracks the speed of the closing door and the sliding cabinet simultaneously, solving a problem no single-focus mind could.

## 11. Still Bouncing, Still Brave

Outside on the grass, safe and sound, Dev and Priya celebrate Zayan. Zayan has his sticker book, but he is still the same energetic, bouncing kid.

*Trait in play:* Zayan is still mid-motion, snapping his rubber band, accepting his neurodivergent brain exactly as it is.
