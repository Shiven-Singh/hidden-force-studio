# The Compass of Oak Creek

**Logline.** A ten-year-old boy with dyslexia uses his unique spatial skills and phonological awareness to navigate a secret maze of school tunnels and find a legendary brass compass.

**Trait causality.** Because Kabir's dyslexia forces him to manually analyze phonemes rather than relying on automatic visual word recognition, he is the only one who can identify the silent letter trap in the word 'shepherd' to unlock the final chest.

## 1. The Golden Goal

Kabir learns about the history hunt and the prize of the Founder's Brass Compass, which he desperately wants to add to his collection of engineering tools.

*Trait in play:* Kabir struggles to spell the word 'trophy' on his team worksheet, writing 'trofee' before correcting it, highlighting his phonological spelling challenges.

## 2. The Gravel Metaphor

Kabir explains his experience of dyslexia to his classmate Chloe after she notices his spelling slip on the team worksheet.

*Trait in play:* Kabir uses the concrete metaphor of walking on loose gravel where his feet slip on the sounds to describe his dyslexia.

## 3. Finding the Clue

Kabir, Chloe, and Leo search the school courtyard and find an old brass plate hidden behind a loose brick.

*Trait in play:* Kabir uses his 3D spatial skills to identify a brick that is physically misaligned with the wall pattern, revealing the hidden plate.

## 4. The Written Hurdle

Leo reads the written clue aloud from the brass plate while Kabir listens and commits every spoken word to his perfect auditory memory.

*Trait in play:* Kabir struggles to correlate the phonemes of the written text on the plate, but his auditory memory allows him to retain the spoken clue instantly.

## 5. The Empty Space

The trio enters the old boiler room corridor, where Kabir notices a physical discrepancy in the layout of the hallway.

*Trait in play:* Kabir uses his folding ruler totem to measure the space, realizing a wall is hollow and finding a hidden lever to open a secret tunnel.

## 6. The Wright Path

The team reaches a fork in the tunnel with doors labeled 'GATE OF THE WRIGHT' and 'GATE OF THE WRITE'.

*Trait in play:* Kabir processes the homophones, recognizing that while they sound identical, they represent different concepts.

## 7. Spatial Over Spelling

Leo tries to choose the door based on visual spelling, but Kabir uses physical geometry to choose the correct path.

*Trait in play:* Kabir's spatial intelligence allows him to map the door to the workshop above, avoiding a dangerous pitfall behind the other door.

## 8. The Silent Trap

They find a chest locked with a three-button phonological puzzle featuring the words 'PHONE', 'SHEPHERD', and 'GHOST'.

*Trait in play:* Kabir faces his anxiety about reading and spelling under pressure but focuses on breaking the words down into their sound components.

## 9. Unlocking the Sound

Kabir analyzes the phonemes of 'shepherd' and realizes the 'h' is silent because the 'p' and 'h' do not form a digraph in this word.

*Trait in play:* Kabir's deep phonological analysis, developed because he must manually process sounds, allows him to solve the puzzle and unlock the chest.

## 10. The Beautiful Mind

The team celebrates their victory, and Kabir writes a journal entry about the adventure, accepting his unique way of reading.

*Trait in play:* Kabir still has dyslexia in the final scene, struggling to spell 'adventure' as 'advencher', but he is confident in his intelligence.
